\section{Data Generation}
%=====================================================================

Demonstrations are produced in two decoupled stages: batched physics
rollouts that record trajectories, followed by a separate rendering
pass that replays them through the splat scene. The physics stage
runs 32--64 parallel environments on one GPU with no renderer in the
loop; rendering is serial and expensive. Decoupling lets us re-render
the same trajectories under new visual randomization without
re-simulating, and apply randomization independently at each stage.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Learning the Expert with RL}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Our first expert was a PPO policy commanding gripper motion
directly. The policy maps a privileged simulator state to an
end-effector delta, executed verbatim at 60\,Hz:
\begin{equation}
    a_t \sim \pi_\theta(s_t), \qquad
    a_t \in [-1,1]^7 = [\Delta p\,(3),\ \Delta r\,(3),\ g\,(1)]
\end{equation}
with $\Delta p$ scaled to $\pm$2\,mm of translation and $\Delta r$
(a rotation vector) to $\pm 0.75^{\circ}$ of rotation per step; the
gripper dimension is kept for format compatibility with the
downstream visuomotor policy but is inert (the grasp is fixed). The
state $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{22}$ concatenates the proprioception the
visuomotor policy will also receive --- end-effector position, 6-D
rotation, gripper --- with privileged terms that images must later
replace: the scaled connector-to-seat error, the rotation error to
the seat frame, and the connector's linear and angular velocities.
The reward is dense progress plus a seated bonus and a contact
penalty,
\begin{equation}
    r_t = w_p \left( d_{t-1} - d_t \right)
        + R_s\, \mathds{1}[\text{seated}_t]
        - w_v\, v_t ,
\end{equation}
where $d_t$ is a weighted distance to the seat pose ($w_p = 100$),
the seated bonus ($R_s = 30$) is velocity-damped, and $v_t$ counts
finger--jack contact frames ($w_v = 2$). Success is hold-based ---
seated within 5\,mm depth, 3\,mm lateral and $8^{\circ}$ angular
error for 20 consecutive steps --- because an instantaneous position
criterion was gameable by a twisted plug. A five-stage curriculum
grows the approach distance (10 to 30\,mm) and the jack's lateral
offset (0 to 8\,mm), with cable droop scaling per stage; the only
environment-side action modification is a standoff clamp capping
forward motion 3\,mm before the jack mouth. The policy is a small
actor--critic MLP ($2\times256$, state-independent log-std) trained
on 64 parallel environments, warm-started by behavioural cloning
from a simple servo routine.

\subsubsection{What Failed}

Getting this to train at all took three runs, each failure
diagnosing a missing ingredient:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Curriculum interacted with the lever arm.} With cable
        droop randomized from the start, the nominally easiest stage
        was already top-stage difficulty --- tilting the cable swings
        the grip point $\sim$19\,mm through the wrist-to-grasp lever
        --- and the first run finished at \textbf{0\,\% held success
        for the entire run}, with the bootstrap actively degrading
        (eval depth shrank from 23 to 10\,mm). Droop had to become a
        curriculum axis.
  \item \textbf{A fresh critic destroys the BC initialization.} The
        first policy update after warm-starting measured a KL of
        0.13 --- garbage advantages from an untrained value function
        wiped out the cloned behaviour immediately. Training the
        critic alone for the first iterations fixed this.
  \item \textbf{Exploration noise is incompatible with a hold
        criterion.} PPO never shrank its action noise on its own
        (entropy stayed at its initial value for 100+ iterations),
        and at $\sigma = 0.29$ the action noise random-walks the plug
        $\sim$2.6\,mm over the 20-step hold window against a 3\,mm
        tolerance --- holds break stochastically no matter how good
        the mean action is. The second run plateaued at $\sim$30\,\%;
        an explicit log-std anneal was the missing piece, after which
        the policy promoted through all five curriculum stages to
        90--94\,\% held success (violation frames falling from
        30\,\% to 1--2\,\%).
\end{itemize}

Even converged, three properties disqualified the policy as a
demonstrator. It parks $\sim$8\,mm past the design seat depth, so
every demonstration ends in a subtly wrong state. It retains
1--2\,\% finger--jack violation frames, forcing rejection filters on
the generated data. And beyond its 96-step training horizon it can
eject violently (78--125$^{\circ}$ of connector twist measured at
$t = 160$--$200$), so episodes must be cut shortly after success. We
also found that evaluation itself required care: a fixed-window
success metric understated performance by $\sim$40 points
(54.5\,\% measured vs.\ 94.8\,\% true steady-state at $\mu = 0.5$)
because it measured insertions still in progress --- success must be
read from a settled tail or a hold counter --- and single-seed
results are unreliable
under the simulator's contact nondeterminism, so every claim is
evaluated over batched rollouts. The policy's one clear advantage is
speed (it seats a 49\,mm-out, $7^{\circ}$-drooping start in
$\sim$40 steps, twice as fast as the scripted controller below), but
speed is not what behavioural cloning needs from a demonstrator.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\columnwidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule}{%
\textcolor{cmured}{\textbf{Remark.}} Due to time constraints we
shifted to the scripted teacher, which is the better
\emph{demonstration writer}: correct terminal state, zero
violations, smooth deterministic actions. Ideally, however, a
working RL expert is the better \emph{teacher}: training visits
off-nominal states and learns to return from them, so its rollouts
--- and DAgger-style relabeling of the student's own drifted states
--- contain exactly the return-to-path supervision a scripted
controller never demonstrates. The recovery-demonstration machinery
we add later reconstructs by hand what an RL expert would have
provided naturally.}}

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Scripted Expert}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Production data instead comes from a scripted align-then-insert
controller: it aligns outside the contact band, rate-limits rotation
to the $0.3^{\circ}$/step the shallow friction grasp can actually
follow, pushes along the insertion axis, and detects jams from
stalled progress. It seats 100\,\% of episodes across all curriculum
stages with zero finger--jack violations and parks at the design
depth ($-0.04$\,mm), so the success gate and violation filters the
PPO data required all become no-ops. Its episodes are
$\sim$2.3$\times$ longer ($\sim$110 vs.\ $\sim$46 frames), which we
treat as a feature: episode length scales with the initial error, so
the dataset covers the whole approach rather than only the final
centimetres. Because the controller is deterministic and its actions
peak near 40\,\% of the per-step caps, we optionally inject
exploration noise into the \emph{executed} action and record what
was executed --- recording the clean command would teach the policy
actions whose consequences it never observed. For hardware-faithful
kinematics the controller is driven through the calibrated servo
chain described in the simulation section.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Recording What the Policy Commands}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

An earlier pipeline replayed the \emph{connector} trajectory and
inferred the gripper from it by per-frame IK. Two independent IK
solves of nominally the same pose disagree, so the recorded
end-effector state jumped at the grasp handoff (rotation steps of
$12$--$24^{\circ}$ against a $7.5^{\circ}$ background), and since
the jump appeared at the same task moment in every episode,
behavioural cloning learned it as intended behaviour: \emph{after
closing the gripper, whip the wrist}. We now record the wrist
trajectory itself --- the quantity the policy commands --- and let
the connector follow its own simulated pose; nothing is inferred.
Trajectories are stored seat-relative in both position and
orientation, which is what makes render-time jack placement jitter
sound: the splat, the replayed trajectory, the IK and the shadows
all move together.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Curation and Label Surgery}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Success is read from a hold counter (consecutive seated frames)
rather than a fixed time window, per the evaluation artifact above.
Only successful episodes are kept, cut shortly after the hold is
established so datasets are not dominated by a seated plug sitting
still.

A known failure mode comes from the expert's pre-insertion
standoff: it decelerates smoothly through the pose where the policy
must eventually push, behavioural cloning reproduces the near-zero
actions there, and the deployed policy stalls or bounces at the
standoff. To counter this we resample episodes by arc length: a
frame is kept only once the accumulated motion since the last kept
frame exceeds a floor (in mm of translation or degrees of
rotation), and action labels are recomputed across the cuts under
the same delta convention, so every label magnitude lands within
one original step of the floor. The first frame, retreat and
recovery segments, gripper transitions, and the terminal frames
(the ``stop at seat'' signal) are always preserved. Whether this
removes the closed-loop stall is still under evaluation.

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Campaign Structure}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Data is generated in seeded chunks: each chunk draws its own grip
point, controller jitter, background yaw and shadow strength, on top
of per-episode randomization of jack yaw, cable droop, grasp roll,
lateral offset and approach distance. Every fifth chunk is a
hard-recovery chunk with jam-and-retreat behaviour enabled, so the
dataset contains recoveries rather than only clean insertions.
Rendered episodes are packaged at 30\,fps with front and wrist
views, a 10-D state (end-effector position, 6-D rotation, gripper)
and 7-D delta actions (translation, rotation vector, gripper),
paired with varied natural-language prompts.

randomization of the 1000-episode set (the grasp roll is centred on
$150^{\circ}$ rather than the nominal $180^{\circ}$ because the
latter sits on a joint-branch boundary of the servo arm).

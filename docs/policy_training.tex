\section{Policy Training and Closed-Loop Evaluation}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Policies}

We train two policy families on the 1000-episode set (and its
arc-length-resampled variant). The first is a pretrained
vision-language-action model ($\pi_{0.5}$) fine-tuned with LoRA
adapters on both the 2B vision-language backbone and the 300M action
expert, conditioned on the front and wrist views ($512^2$), the 10-D
proprioceptive state, and a language prompt; action horizon 10
(later 16), batch size 32, cosine learning rate peaking at
$5\times10^{-5}$, 15--30k steps. The second is an ACT transformer
($\sim$51M parameters) trained from scratch on the same data at
chunk sizes 10 and 100, executing each predicted chunk in full
before re-observing; it is not language-conditioned. Both consume
identical observations through the same evaluation harness, so
differences in closed-loop behaviour are attributable to
architecture and data variant rather than the interface.

\subsection{Open-Loop Probe: Learning Is Not the Problem}

Before closed-loop evaluation we probe checkpoints on
training-distribution frames, comparing the first predicted action
against the expert's recorded action. Learning proceeds steadily:
translation error falls from 0.274 to 0.088\,mm and the action
direction cosine rises from 0.59 to 0.85 between 5k and 20k steps
(plateauing near 12.5k), and one-step predictions sit on the expert
path. Whatever fails downstream, it is not single-step imitation.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Results}

Evaluation runs the full live pipeline --- the servo-plant physics
environment with the fixture, campaign visuals including shadows
(required for observation parity), and the same artifact-cleanup
model in the loop. Across held-out start seeds \emph{no policy seats
the connector} ($0/24$ for $\pi_{0.5}$ at step 10k). Under the most
favourable conditions --- replaying the exact conditions of a
training episode --- Table~\ref{tab:nc-closedloop} records each
model's closest approach.

Three observations. First, the standoff stall is partly
architecture-specific: ACT trained on the \emph{same} original data
does not stall, while $\pi_{0.5}$ does, so the pause pathology in
the data is amplified by, not solely responsible for, the behaviour.
Second, arc-length resampling removes the stall but trades it for
boldness without precision: the resampled ACT reaches a nearly
perfect pre-dock pose ($3.0$\,mm lateral) and then charges forward,
losing lateral lock --- the resampling floor evidently removed the
slow fine-alignment regime along with the pauses. Third,
\textcolor{cmured}{every model misses laterally} (at best
$5.8$\,mm against the $\sim$3\,mm bore).

\subsection{Diagnosis: Compounding, Not Perception}

The probe and the closed-loop table together isolate the failure:
one-step predictions are accurate, but integrating the policy's own
deltas drifts 4--6\,mm within 40 frames \emph{even when
teacher-forced on ground-truth observations} --- already beyond the
bore tolerance. This is classic behavioural-cloning compounding: the
expert data contains no return-to-path behaviour, so once off-axis
every model stays off. This is precisely the supervision an RL
teacher would have provided (see the remark in the data-generation
section), and it motivates the next campaign: recovery
demonstrations. A validated pilot generates them two ways ---
off-nominal starts placed directly in the pre-dock failure zone
(4--16\,mm lateral, where every recorded frame is corrective), and
injected lateral perturbations during alignment, with episodes split
at the perturbation so that labels remain consistent pose deltas ---
alongside denser coverage of the terminal phase.
